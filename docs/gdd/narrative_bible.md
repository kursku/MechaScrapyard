# Mecha Scrapyard — Narrative Bible
## The Complete Story Foundation

---

## 1. The Core Story in One Paragraph

You are the child of Kazuo Hayashi, a decorated New Tokyo police officer and mecha pilot who was accused of corruption and disappeared before his trial. The government seized everything — your home, your savings, your future. You were forced to move in with your grandfather, Ichiro Hayashi, who runs a crumbling scrapyard at the edge of the city. While working the junk piles, you discover your father's first mecha — a frame he built by hand when he was your age, rusting in the back of the garage. You restore it. You learn to pilot it. And piece by piece, fight by fight, you uncover the truth: your father wasn't corrupt. He was investigating something so dangerous that powerful people needed him gone. Now you're asking the same questions he asked. And the same people are watching.

---

## 2. The Three Hayashis

### Ichiro Hayashi — The Grandfather ("Grandpa")

**Who he is:** A retired mechanical engineer who spent forty years running the scrapyard. Stubborn, practical, deeply caring beneath a gruff exterior. He built the scrapyard from nothing and understands machines the way a poet understands words. He never wanted Kazuo to join the police — he wanted him in the shop, building things. Their relationship was strained by that choice.

**What he knows:** More than he says. He knew Kazuo modified mechas "off the books" in the garage late at night. He knew Kazuo was involved in something beyond police work. He didn't know about Phantom Works specifically — that revelation (Phase 4, Dad's Last Route) genuinely shocks him. He kept the garage sealed not out of suspicion, but out of grief.

**His role in the game:** Grandpa is the player's anchor. He narrates the first mission, the last mission, and the emotional peaks in between. His voice shifts across the game:
- Phase 1-2: Protective, cautious. "Don't go looking for trouble."
- Phase 3: Reluctantly proud. "You fight like him. That's not a compliment."
- Phase 4: Concerned. "This is what got your father in trouble."
- Phase 5: Accepting. "You're ready. Go see what he left behind."
- Dad's Secret: Broken open. "He asked me to keep it secret. He said: 'When the kid is ready, the lock will open.'"

**His relationship with the scrapyard:** The scrapyard is Ichiro's life's work. Every upgrade the player builds is an extension of his legacy — the Garage is his design, the Workshop his tools, the Hangars his ambition from decades ago. When the player reaches Phase 5, the scrapyard has become what Ichiro always dreamed it could be. He just never thought it would take his grandson fighting a war to get there.

**His death (The Last Stand — msn_scrapyard_siege, Phase 5):**
When the player traces Dad's Last Route and activates a Phantom Works transponder, the conspiracy realizes the investigation is alive again. Taeyang Syndicate sends an enforcer squad to the scrapyard — not to capture, but to destroy. Burn the evidence. Erase the Hayashi problem permanently.

Grandpa is at the scrapyard when they arrive. The player isn't.

Ichiro doesn't run. He barricades the workshop door with his own workbench — the one he built forty years ago — and stands between the thermal rounds and the floor panel that hides Kazuo's Secret Lab. He doesn't know exactly what's down there. But he knows his son built it, and he knows it matters.

By the time the player arrives and fights off the remaining attackers, it's already done. Grandpa is behind the workshop. Burned hands. A thermal round through the wall. He was standing in front of the lab entrance.

His last words reveal the lab's existence:
> "Under... the workshop. He made me promise. The lock... it's keyed to you."
> "I told him it was reckless. Building all that in secret." A pause. A breath that costs everything. "He said some things are worth being reckless for."
> "He was right."

He squeezes the player's hand once. Then the workshop is very quiet.

**Why the sacrifice works narratively:**
- Grandpa is the one character who was always safe. His death shatters the player's foundation.
- He dies protecting something he barely understood — an act of pure faith in his son's work.
- His dying words ("some things are worth being reckless for") echo Dad's recorded message, which the player hasn't heard yet. When they enter the lab and hear Dad say the exact same phrase, the connection between father and grandfather — two men who disagreed about everything except what mattered — lands with devastating force.
- Dad's final words in the recording: "Take care of Grandpa." The player hears this after Grandpa is already gone. There is nothing to say. There is only the work.
- The scrapyard, which was always warmth and safety, becomes a monument. Every structure the player built is now a memorial. The idle loop continues, but the flavor changes: the workshop hums, but no one is tinkering at the bench.

**Post-death:** Grandpa never speaks again as a mission narrator. His voice is replaced by silence, system messages, and the absence where his gruff advice used to be. The Arena Championship debriefing places an empty seat in the front row with a wrench on it — the Underground's tribute. The scrapyard events that referenced Grandpa ("Grandpa nods approvingly") are replaced with quiet observations about tools left where he placed them, routines that continue without him.

### Kazuo Hayashi — The Father ("Dad")

**Who he was:**
- A decorated officer of the New Tokyo Police Department (NTPD), specializing in mecha-related crime
- A gifted mecha pilot — one of the best in the department
- An engineer by passion — self-taught, building and modifying frames in his father's scrapyard after hours
- A man who believed in justice, but discovered that the institutions of justice were compromised

**The official story:** Officer Kazuo Hayashi was charged with corruption — specifically, accepting bribes from corporate entities to look the other way on illegal weapons shipments. Before the trial could begin, he vanished. The government, under New Tokyo's asset forfeiture laws, seized his home, accounts, and all personal property. His family was left with nothing. The case was closed. File sealed.

**The real story (revealed across Phases 3-5):**
Kazuo discovered that Taeyang Forge was running illegal thermal weapons through the industrial docks, but the operation had protection — from inside the NTPD, from Aegis-Tac corporate security, and possibly from Kuroda Military Command. When he tried to report it through official channels, he was warned to stop. When he didn't stop, the corruption charges were fabricated.

Desperate, Kazuo made contact with the Phantom Collective — the remnants of Phantom Works, a rogue engineering group that had been dismantled by corporate interests years earlier. They shared a common enemy: the corporate-military machine that controlled New Tokyo from the shadows. Kazuo began working with them in secret, using the scrapyard garage as his hidden workshop.

His project: the Hayabusa Mk.II — a Light-class frame with specs that rivaled Heavy military units. If completed, it would prove that independent engineering could match corporate military power. It was both a weapon and a statement: you don't need a corporation to build something extraordinary.

He disappeared before finishing it. Whether he was captured, killed, or went into hiding is the game's central mystery — one that the player may never fully resolve, because the answer matters less than what the player does with what he left behind.

**What he left behind:**
- The Hayabusa Mk.I: his first frame, built when he was the player's age. Rusted, damaged, tier 1. The player's starting mecha.
- The Secret Lab: hidden beneath the scrapyard workshop, biometric-locked to his child's DNA. Contains blueprints, tools, materials, and a recorded message.
- A network: contacts in Phantom Works, the Underground, and even within the NTPD who remember him. The player inherits these connections, whether they know it or not.
- A reputation: depending on who you ask, Kazuo Hayashi was a hero, a traitor, a fool, or a ghost. The player's actions redefine what his name means.

**His recorded message (Dad's Secret mission, Phase 5):**
> "I'm sorry I can't be there. But everything I know is here. Everything I built is for you. The Mk.II isn't finished — I ran out of time. The blueprints are complete. The materials are cataloged. You'll need help. Find the people I worked with. They'll know who you are.
> Finish what I started. And take care of Grandpa.
> He's going to say I was reckless. He's right. But some things are worth being reckless for."

### The Player — The Child

**Who they are:** A young person (late teens/early twenties) who lost everything when their father was accused and disappeared. No formal training — not a pilot, not an engineer, not a fighter. What they have is stubbornness (from Grandpa), instinct (from Dad), and nothing left to lose.

**Their arc:** From victim to agent. Phase by phase:
- **Phase 1-2:** Survival. Learning the scrapyard. Doing what Grandpa says.
- **Phase 3:** Agency. Fighting for the first time. Discovering they're good at it. Choosing to investigate.
- **Phase 4:** Identity. Becoming known in New Tokyo. Making enemies. Learning that Dad was more than what the files say.
- **Phase 5:** Legacy. Finishing Dad's work. Confronting the powers that destroyed the family. Deciding what the Hayashi name means going forward.

**The player's central choice (not yet implemented but seeded):** When the truth about Dad is fully revealed, the player must decide what to do with it. Expose the corporate conspiracy publicly (Idealist path)? Use the information as leverage for power (Pragmatist path)? Or something in between? This is where the morality system fully activates.

## 4. The Mystery — Layer by Layer

The father's disappearance is not a single revelation but a series of nested mysteries, each answer opening a deeper question:

### Layer 1 — The Surface (Phase 1-3)
**What the player knows:** Dad was a cop. He was accused of corruption. He disappeared. We lost everything.
**What the player assumes:** He was probably guilty. Or maybe innocent but couldn't prove it. Either way, he's gone.
**What the player discovers:** Dad built mechas in the garage. He was an engineer, not just a cop. There are sealed files about him.

### Layer 2 — The Investigation (Phase 3-4)
**What the player learns:** Dad was investigating illegal weapons — specifically Taeyang thermal weapons moving through the docks. The investigation was shut down from above. The corruption charges appeared immediately after he filed his report.
**New question:** Who had the power to fabricate charges against a decorated officer?

### Layer 3 — The Conspiracy (Phase 4)
**What the player learns:** The weapons operation had protection from multiple sources — NTPD internal affairs, Aegis-Tac corporate security, and possibly Kuroda Military Command. Dad didn't just stumble onto a smuggling ring. He found a **network** connecting corporate, military, and law enforcement interests.
**New question:** If official channels were compromised, who did Dad turn to?

### Layer 4 — The Alliance (Phase 4-5)
**What the player learns:** Dad made contact with the Phantom Collective — exiled engineers and hackers who opposed the corporate-military establishment. He began working with them in secret, using the scrapyard as cover. He was building something — the Hayabusa Mk.II — as proof that independent innovation could rival corporate military power.
**New question:** Why did he disappear? Was he caught? Did he run? Is he alive?

### Layer 5 — The Truth (Phase 5)
**What the player finds:** The Secret Lab. The blueprints. The recorded message. Dad's voice, explaining everything. He wasn't corrupt — he was the one honest cop in a system designed to protect the powerful. He ran out of time.
**What remains unanswered (intentionally):** Where is Dad now? The game does not resolve this. The recorded message was made before his disappearance. The player inherits his mission, his tools, and his unanswered questions. Some stories don't end with answers. They end with the next generation picking up the work.

## 5. Faction Relationships with the Father

Each faction has a specific, narratively coherent relationship with Kazuo Hayashi. These relationships shape how the faction treats the player:

### NTPD — New Tokyo Police Department
**Relationship:** Former employer. Complicated.
**What they know:** Kazuo was one of their best officers. The corruption charges divided the department — some believe he was guilty, others think he was framed. His case file is sealed by order of Internal Affairs.
**How they treat the player:** With suspicion and curiosity. The Hayashi name opens some doors (sympathetic officers who remember Kazuo) and closes others (administrators who want the case to stay buried). The player carrying the name of an accused-corrupt officer creates tension at every level.
**Key NPC connections:** The Aegis officer in Corporate Warning recognizes the frame signature — Aegis-Tac supplied equipment to the NTPD, and Kazuo's modifications were logged in their system.
**Narrative thread:** At high rep (Allied+), an NTPD officer quietly provides the player with Kazuo's sealed case file — the first concrete evidence that the charges were fabricated.

### Kuroda Military Command
**Relationship:** Part of the conspiracy (unknowingly or knowingly).
**What they know:** Kazuo's investigation touched Kuroda's weapons contracts. Whether Kuroda actively participated in his downfall or was simply used as a shield by Taeyang is ambiguous. Commander Steele specifically remembers Kazuo — his report crossed her desk before it was classified.
**How they treat the player:** As a potential threat that needs assessment. Steele's "Was his name—" in the boss fight is not hostile — it's recognition. She respected Kazuo even if her institution didn't.
**Narrative thread:** At Operative rep, Steele privately shares what she knows: "Your father's report was legitimate. I verified it. Then it disappeared from the system. I don't know who erased it, but it wasn't us."

### The Underground
**Relationship:** Allied through the grandfather.
**What they know:** Ichiro has been part of the Underground trading network for decades. Kazuo used the scrapyard to modify mechas off the books — some for police use, some for Underground contacts. He was respected for his engineering, not his badge.
**How they treat the player:** As family. The scrapyard is known in Underground circles. Older fixers remember "Ichiro's kid" and extend goodwill to the grandchild. Iron Volk, the raider warlord, specifically targeted the scrapyard because he knew about its hidden value — the word on the street is that something important is buried there.
**Narrative thread:** At Connected rep, an Underground fixer reveals that Kazuo paid them to hide emergency supplies and false documents in a Neon Bazaar dead drop — "in case something went wrong." It went wrong.

### Taeyang Syndicate
**Relationship:** The enemy. Confirmed.
**What they know:** Kazuo was investigating their illegal weapons shipments. When the investigation threatened to expose the protection network, the syndicate's corporate arm activated the response: fabricated charges, sealed files, a convenient disappearance. Years later, when the player activates a Phantom Works transponder while tracing Dad's route, the syndicate realizes the investigation is alive again. They send an enforcer squad to the scrapyard — not to warn, but to destroy. The attack kills Grandpa.
**How they treat the player:** Before the siege: with cautious interest. After the siege: the relationship is defined by what they took. Crimson Nail doesn't know or care about the details — she's operational. But the Taeyang leadership knows they killed an old man to protect a secret, and the old man's grandchild is now coming for answers.
**Narrative thread:** At Enforcer rep, a Taeyang insider (motivated by their own grievances) leaks a partial document: the original order to "resolve the Hayashi situation." It doesn't specify how. At the highest rep levels, the player can discover that the siege order was given by someone OUTSIDE Taeyang — suggesting the syndicate was a weapon, not the hand that aimed it.

### Phantom Collective
**Relationship:** Partners. The deepest connection.
**What they know:** Everything. Kazuo came to them because he had nowhere else to go. They shared intelligence, resources, and a vision: proving that the corporate-military machine wasn't invincible. The Hayabusa Mk.II was their joint project — Kazuo's engineering genius combined with Phantom's bleeding-edge technology.
**How they treat the player:** With hope and caution. Null tests the player not out of cruelty but out of necessity — Kazuo's child must be strong enough to carry what he left behind. The Phantom Collective has been waiting for someone to pick up Kazuo's work. They're patient. They're also desperate.
**Narrative thread:** The entire Phase 5 arc is the Phantom thread. Null's dialogue is the most revealing: "Your father was building something for us. Something important. He disappeared before he could finish. We've been waiting for someone to pick up where he left off."

## 6. The Scrapyard as Narrative Space

The scrapyard is not just a base — it's a physical manifestation of the Hayashi family's history. Each phase of its restoration echoes a phase of the story:

| Phase | Scrapyard State | Narrative Parallel |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1** | Abandoned. Dust and rust. Barely functional. | The family after Kazuo's disappearance. Broken. |
| **Phase 2** | Workshop restored. Power returns. Things work again. | The player choosing to rebuild instead of accepting loss. |
| **Phase 3** | Garage opens. Dad's frame discovered. Combat begins. | The player stepping into Dad's world. No longer just surviving. |
| **Phase 4** | Hangar operational. Trading hub active. Known entity. | The player becoming what Dad was: an independent force in New Tokyo. |
| **Phase 5** | Massive hangar. Secret Lab found. Full capability. But the workshop is empty. | Grandpa's sacrifice. The scrapyard is what Ichiro dreamed and Kazuo needed — but Ichiro isn't here to see it. The family legacy, complete and broken at once. |

### Key Spaces and Their Meanings

**The Workshop (Grandpa's domain):** Tools worn smooth by forty years of use. Every wrench has a story. This is Ichiro's identity — the place where things get fixed. When the player upgrades the Workshop, they're honoring Grandpa's life's work.

**The Garage (Dad's domain):** Sealed for years after Kazuo disappeared. When the player opens it, the air smells like machine oil and old memories. The Mk.I is inside, covered in a tarp. Ichiro sealed it not because he was suspicious of Kazuo, but because he couldn't bear to look at it. Opening the garage is the game's first major emotional beat.

**The Secret Lab (Dad's hidden legacy):** Beneath the workshop floor, behind a biometric lock keyed to the player's DNA. Kazuo built it in secret over years. Even Ichiro didn't know the full extent — he knew about the space, but not what was inside. The Lab contains: Mk.II blueprints, Phantom Works communication equipment, research notes, material stockpiles, and the recorded message. Finding the Lab is the game's climactic emotional beat.

## 7. Speaker Voice Guide

Each narrative speaker has a distinct voice that remains consistent across all missions and events:

### Grandpa (Ichiro Hayashi)
**Tone:** Warm but gruff. Says things once. Doesn't repeat himself. Shows love through worry and practical advice, not sentiment. Uses short sentences. Occasionally lets emotion crack through — these moments are powerful because they're rare.
**Final appearance:** msn_scrapyard_siege. His last spoken lines in the game.
**Speech patterns:**
- Tends to start with observations: "There's a drone circling the perimeter."
- Avoids direct praise: "You did it" instead of "You were amazing."
- References Dad through comparison: "You fight like him."
- When emotional, gets quieter, not louder: "He never told me."

**Examples:**
- Protective: "Don't go looking for trouble. Trouble knows where we live."
- Proud (hiding it): "The frame responded to you. ...That's not a compliment. He was reckless."
- Grief: "I kept the garage sealed. Not because of what's inside. Because of who isn't."
- Acceptance: "You're ready. Go see what he left behind."
- Final words: "He said some things are worth being reckless for. He was right."

**Post-death presence:** After msn_scrapyard_siege, Grandpa is absent. No narrator credits. No dialogue. His presence is conveyed through objects: tools left where he placed them, the burn marks on the workshop wall, the wrench on the empty arena seat. The game never replaces him with another mentor figure. The silence IS the tribute.

### System Narrator
**Tone:** Cold, technical, factual. No emotion. Uses military-style formatting. Represents the world as data — zones, alerts, tactical assessments. The contrast between System's clinical voice and the emotional content it delivers creates tension.
**Speech patterns:**
- ALL CAPS for alerts: "ZONE ALERT: Entering Corporate Perimeter."
- Technical language: "Multiple frame signatures detected."
- Recommendations, not orders: "Recommendation: Upgrade equipment before proceeding."
- Occasionally delivers emotional content flatly, which makes it hit harder: "NAVIGATION LOG DECRYPTED — Origin: [DAD'S FRAME ID]"

### Arena Master
**Tone:** Enthusiastic, performative, slightly comedic. Loves the spectacle. Treats everything like entertainment. Provides levity in a game that can get heavy. Calls the player "kid" or "fresh meat" early, shifts to respect over time.
**Speech patterns:**
- Exclamation marks. Energy: "Fresh meat! Love it!"
- Self-aware humor: "Loser walks out too — we're not animals. Just... enthusiasts."
- Genuine respect when earned: "The crowd liked you. You're in."
- The Championship debriefing breaks pattern — quieter, more human: "Your grandfather is in the crowd. He's not saying anything. But he's smiling."

### NPC Speakers (one-off or rare)
**Aegis Officer:** Procedural. Cold. Speaks in regulations. "Attention, unlicensed pilot."
**Bazaar Contact:** Pragmatic. Shady. Values-neutral. "Interested?" is their favorite word.
**Phantom Contact (Null):** Mysterious. Knowing. Speaks like someone who's been watching for a long time. "So you're his kid. You have his eyes."
**Steele:** Professional. Clipped. Military. But with a crack of humanity underneath: "You have talent. Wasted talent, but talent."

## 8. Thematic Pillars

### Legacy is not inherited — it's rebuilt.
The player doesn't receive Dad's power. They receive his broken frame, his sealed lab, and his unfinished work. Everything must be earned through effort, growth, and choice. The Hayabusa Mk.I starts as the worst frame in the game. The Mk.II requires mastery of every system. Legacy isn't a gift. It's a challenge.

### The truth doesn't set you free — it gives you a choice.
Discovering that Dad was framed doesn't resolve anything. It opens new questions and forces decisions. What do you do with dangerous knowledge? Who do you trust? How far are you willing to go? The morality system exists because the truth alone isn't enough — what matters is what you do with it.

### Family is the constant — until it isn't.
Corporations rise and fall. Factions shift allegiance. The Arena crowd cheers whoever wins. But Grandpa is always at the scrapyard. Dad's message is always in the lab. The family's tools are always in the workshop. In a world defined by power and corruption, the Hayashi scrapyard is the one place that stays real. Until Phase 5, when Grandpa dies and even that certainty is taken away. After the siege, the scrapyard is still home — but it's a home with an empty chair. The tools remain. The routines continue. But the person who made it all feel safe is gone. This loss is what transforms the player from someone investigating the past into someone fighting for the future. Family isn't the constant. What family *built* is the constant.

### You are not your father.
The game constantly compares the player to Kazuo — Grandpa does it, enemies do it, Phantom does it. But the player's choices diverge from Dad's path. Kazuo was a cop who became a rogue engineer. The player is a scrapyard kid who becomes... whatever they choose. The comparison is the starting point, not the destination.

### The fail-forward philosophy.
From the tabletop roots: every loss teaches something. Every defeat reveals narrative. Every broken part becomes materials for something better. The scrapyard itself embodies this — everything here was someone else's failure, repurposed into someone else's opportunity. The game's mechanical design (dual debriefings, partial rewards on defeat, diminishing returns nudging forward) reflects this philosophy at every level.

## 9. The Death of Ichiro Hayashi

The most devastating narrative beat in the game. Grandpa dies in Phase 5, during the Scrapyard Siege (msn_scrapyard_siege). His death is a sacrifice — deliberate, conscious, and irreversible.

### What Happens

After the player confronts Null and learns the full truth about Dad's work with the Phantom Collective, Taeyang retaliates. They send a corporate strike team to the scrapyard — unmarked frames, no IFF transponders, surgical precision. They know what's hidden under the workshop. They want it destroyed.

Grandpa is home. The player is not.

By the time the player arrives, the workshop is burning. Grandpa has dragged his workbench — the one he built forty years ago — across the hidden floor panel. A barricade. His hands are burned from the fire suppressor. A thermal round punched through the wall. He was standing between it and the staircase.

He knew. He always knew where the lab was. He kept the secret for years. And when the moment came, he didn't run. He didn't hide. He stood between the attack and his son's legacy.

### His Last Words

**Victory version** (player drives off the attackers):
> "Under... the workshop. He made me promise. The lock... it's keyed to you."
> "I told him it was reckless. Building all that in secret."
> A pause. A breath that costs everything.
> "He said some things are worth being reckless for."
> "He was right."
> Grandpa's hand finds yours. Squeezes once.
> Then the workshop is very quiet.

**Defeat version** (player is overwhelmed, Underground reinforcements arrive):
> "Under the floor. Don't let them take it."
> He doesn't make it through the night. The medics say the smoke inhalation was too severe.

Both paths converge: Grandpa is gone. The floor panel is intact. The lab awaits.

### Why It Works Narratively

**Sacrifice, not victimhood.** Grandpa doesn't die because he's old or weak. He dies because he CHOSE to protect the lab. He could have evacuated. He chose not to. This is agency, not tragedy — and it echoes the game's core theme: legacy is not inherited, it's actively defended.

**The echo.** Grandpa's last words — "some things are worth being reckless for" — are the SAME words Dad says in his recorded message in the lab. The player hasn't heard Dad's message yet. When they descend into the lab and hear Dad say those exact words, the emotional impact doubles. The father and grandfather, separated by years and circumstance, said the same thing. The Hayashi philosophy, passed down through actions, not words.

**The empty seat.** After Grandpa's death, the Arena Championship mission's briefing references him: "Ichiro Hayashi watched every one of your fights from the back row. Never cheered. Never said a word. Just watched." The victory debriefing: "You're looking at the back row. The seat where Grandpa used to sit. It's empty." This is grief without sentimentality — the game doesn't tell you to be sad. It shows you absence.

**"Take care of Grandpa."** Dad's recorded message ends with this line. It was recorded years before the siege. Dad couldn't know what would happen. The player can't fulfill this request. This is the most devastating line in the game — not because of what it says, but because of what the player knows and Dad doesn't.

### Impact on Game Systems

| System | Change |
|---|---|
| Grandpa as speaker | No new missions use Grandpa as narrator after the siege. His voice is gone from the game. The silence is the point. |
| Scrapyard events | Post-siege events reflect the absence: "The workshop is quieter now. The tools are where he left them." |
| Morality | The siege itself doesn't shift morality. What the player does AFTER — revenge vs. justice vs. pragmatism — does. |
| Secret Lab | Unlocks immediately after siege. Grandpa's sacrifice is the key. |
| Arena Championship | Explicitly references Grandpa's death. His empty seat. The Arena Master's rare moment of sincerity. |

### Design Notes

Grandpa dies regardless of whether the player wins or loses the siege combat. The sacrifice already happened before the player arrived. Victory means you drove off the attackers and were there for his last words. Defeat means the Underground saved you, but Grandpa was already gone. The difference is intimacy, not outcome.

No scrap. No creds. No loot. This mission awards nothing material. Just 20 glory — the weight of loss — and the unlock to Dad's Secret. The game's economy pauses for grief. Then continues. Because that's what Grandpa would have wanted.

## 10. Narrative Gaps (Intentional)

Some questions are deliberately left unanswered to preserve mystery and enable future content:

| Question | Status | Why It's Open |
|---|---|---|
| Is Kazuo alive? | Unknown | The recorded message was made before his disappearance. No body was found. Keeping this open preserves hope and narrative tension. |
| Who specifically ordered the frame-up? | Partially revealed | Taeyang Syndicate activated the response, but who gave the final order? A corporate board? A politician? A military general? The scrapyard siege order may also have originated from outside Taeyang — suggesting a higher power pulling strings. This is expansion content. |
| What was the Mk.II's ultimate purpose? | Partially revealed | Phantom says it was "something important." Dad says "finish what I started." But what exactly? A weapon? A proof of concept? A key to something else? The blueprints reveal the specs, not the full intent. |
| Did Grandpa know more than he says? | Ambiguous | He knew about the space under the workshop. He kept the garage sealed. How much did Kazuo tell him? Ichiro's grief is genuine, but his knowledge may be deeper than he admits. |

## 11. Naming Conventions

### The Hayashi Family
- **Kazuo Hayashi** — the father. "Kazuo" means "harmonious man" or "first-born son" in Japanese. Ironic for a man whose life became anything but harmonious.
- **Ichiro Hayashi** — the grandfather. "Ichiro" means "first son." A traditional name for a traditional man.
- **The Player** — unnamed by design. The player IS the character. No predetermined name enforces identification.
- **Hayashi** — the family name. It means "grove" or "forest" in Japanese — fitting for a family rooted in one place (the scrapyard) while the city grows around them.

### Hayabusa
- "Hayabusa" means "peregrine falcon" in Japanese — the fastest bird in the world. Dad named his engineering project after speed, precision, and freedom. A peregrine falcon doesn't need to be big to be powerful. Neither does a Light frame.
- **Hayabusa Engineering** is not a corporation. It's a label Dad put on his personal work — a name for his dreams. When the player crafts the Mk.II, they're not building a product. They're completing a dream.

### New Tokyo
- A deliberate fusion of Japanese tradition and cyberpunk futurism. The city remembers what it was (Tokyo) while being defined by what it became (New). This duality — old and new, tradition and progress, memory and ambition — runs through every district, faction, and character.
